Wilma privacy policy

Last updated 8 October 2026. Wilma is a personal assistant app. Contact: zaftechlabs@gmail.com

Wilma keeps the notes, files and passwords you give it and finds them again when you ask. This page explains what is stored, where, who can see it, and how to have it deleted.

What Wilma stores

  • Your account: your email address and your sign-in password (stored only as a one-way hash by our sign-in provider).
  • What you save: spaces, notes, photos and files you add, their titles, tags and descriptions, and earlier versions of notes you edit. A place note can also hold an address, a Google Maps link and, if you choose, the location where you saved it (see below). Tasks you add (what, how long, where, by when) and your Home place, if you set one for planning your day, are saved the same way, as your notes.
  • Your vault: passwords, keys and other secrets you save in the vault. Their values are encrypted on your phone (or in your browser) with a key only you hold, before they are sent. The name, kind and website you give each secret are stored readable, so you can find it; a log records when each secret is created, revealed, changed or deleted.
  • Technical records: our hosting provider keeps short-lived server logs (for example IP address, time and the address requested) to run and protect the service.
  • Chat usage: the number of chat requests you make each month and their cost. Your chat conversations themselves are kept only on your phone; clearing the chat in the app forgets them.

Wilma has no advertising, no analytics and no tracking, and does not collect your contacts. Location: Wilma reads your phone's location only when you tap Save where I am or Use where I am now on a place or for your Home, or 📍 in the chat, or 🔎 Find it on the map on a place you shared (that tap only asks for the permission; your location is not read). It asks for permission on that first tap, reads the location once, and stores it in that place note only, like the rest of the note; you can remove it from the note at any time. A place you share from Google Maps can also get a location from that place's name and address (never from where you are), after you confirm it. In the chat, if you tap 📍, Wilma reads your location once and sends it with that one message so she can find your saved places nearby. Like the message itself, it goes to our AI service for that answer only; it is not saved or logged. Wilma never reads your location in the background and never tracks where you go. The camera, the microphone and file access are used only when you choose to: the camera and files when you add a photo or file, the microphone when you tap the microphone button to dictate a message. Dictation uses your phone's own speech recognition. Depending on your phone, it runs on the phone or your phone's speech service (on most Android phones, Google) receives the audio to turn it into text, under that service's own privacy terms. Wilma receives only the resulting text, never the audio, and does not record or keep audio.

Your calendar (optional). If you turn on "Use my calendar", Wilma reads the calendars you choose on your phone, only when you ask about your day. It sends the event titles, times and places needed for that answer to its AI provider, never descriptions, attendees or meeting links, and private events only as "Busy". Wilma does not store your calendar and does not change it. You can turn it off, or untick calendars, at any time in Settings.

Planning your day (optional, Wilma Pro). When you open My day or ask Wilma to plan your day, Wilma works out drive times and weather for the places on your calendar and your tasks. To know where an event is, your phone looks up the event's address with its own map lookup (see below), only if you have already allowed location for Wilma; the event's title is not sent. Wilma then sends those places' map coordinates and the times you would travel to Mapbox (for drive times with traffic) and to the US National Weather Service (for the forecast), never event titles or who is going. Your Home place and your tasks are saved with your notes. The plan itself is not stored, and Wilma never changes your calendar. Your own choices for the day ("take both", "not driving") and your answers to "where is this?" are kept only on your phone, encrypted, and are forgotten when you sign out.

Who can see it

  • Only your account can use your data. Every request runs as you, and the database refuses access to other accounts' data.
  • Vault values: nobody but you can read them, including us. If you lose both your vault passphrase and your recovery key, they cannot be recovered.
  • Notes and files are encrypted at rest by our hosting provider but are not end-to-end encrypted. The people who run the service could technically access them; we do not look at them except to fix a problem you report, or when the law requires it.
  • We do not sell, rent or share your data with anyone for their own use.

Services we use

  • Supabase (database, file storage and sign-in), hosted in the United States (Oregon). It stores the data described above on our behalf.
  • GitHub Pages hosts this page and the vault web pages. Expo builds the app; it receives no data about you from the app.
  • OpenAI (the AI model behind Wilma's chat), in the United States. When you use the chat, what you type, the recent conversation, the notes and vault entry names Wilma looks up to answer, and, when you ask about your day, the calendar events needed for that answer are sent to OpenAI to write the reply. Vault values are never sent: Wilma never sees them. We ask OpenAI not to store these requests, and OpenAI does not use them to train its models. OpenAI may keep them for up to 30 days to detect abuse, then deletes them.
  • Your phone's speech service (on most Android phones, Google's), only when you tap the microphone button to dictate. It turns your speech into text under its own privacy terms; Wilma gets only the text, which is then treated exactly like text you typed.
  • Google Maps, only when you tap Open in Maps on a place or Directions in My day: your phone opens Google Maps (the app, or the website) with that place's saved link, address or location, under Google's own privacy terms. Wilma's servers do not contact Google about your places.
  • Your phone's map lookup (on most Android phones, Google's), only when you share a place from Google Maps into Wilma: the phone sends that place's name and address to find where it is, and then the spot it found, to show you its street and town, so Wilma can ask "Is this it?". The lookup happens under your phone's own privacy terms, and nothing is saved unless you confirm. Your own location is not sent. When you plan your day, the same lookup finds the addresses of your calendar events (the address text only, never the event's title), and the addresses you type for your Home or a task.
  • Mapbox, in the United States, only when you plan your day: Wilma's server sends it the map coordinates of the places you drive between and when you would leave, to get drive times with traffic. Nothing else about you is sent, and Wilma does not keep the answers.
  • The US National Weather Service (api.weather.gov), only when you plan your day: Wilma's server sends it the rounded map coordinates of the places you drive to, to get the hourly forecast and weather alerts there. Nothing else about you is sent.
  • If you choose to connect Wilma to an AI assistant such as Claude, the things you ask it to look up or save pass through that assistant under your own account with it and its privacy policy.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data is kept while your account exists. You can delete notes (they go to a recycle bin first, then can be deleted for good), files, spaces and vault secrets in the app at any time. To delete your whole account and everything in it, see how to delete your account. We complete deletions within 30 days.

Children

Wilma is not meant for children under 13, and we do not knowingly hold their data.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is posted here with a new date. Features that send data somewhere new are described here before they are switched on.
